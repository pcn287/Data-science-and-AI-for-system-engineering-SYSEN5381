The livestock monitoring system recorded a total of 73 animals with 1,802 visits, resulting in a combined emission of 554,525.78 grams of CH4 and 22,090,365.64 grams of CO2. The data indicates significant variability in methane emissions among individual animals, highlighting potential targets for emission reduction strategies.

## Insights
- The top emitters account for a substantial portion of total CH4 emissions, suggesting that targeted interventions could lead to significant reductions.
- There is a correlation between the number of visits and CH4 emissions; animals with higher visit counts tend to have higher emissions, which may indicate dietary or health-related factors influencing methane production.
- A small number of animals contribute disproportionately to total emissions, indicating potential for focused management strategies.

## Recommendations
- Implement targeted feeding strategies or dietary adjustments for the top emitters to reduce methane production.
- Increase monitoring frequency for high-emission animals to better understand their dietary habits and health status, enabling more effective management interventions.
