# C-Lock GreenFeeder Emissions Data Analysis

## Overview
This report summarizes the emissions data collected from the C-Lock GreenFeeder for FeederID 453 between August 1, 2025, and November 1, 2025. The analysis focuses on key metrics such as CO2, CH4, and other gas emissions during feeding sessions.

## Key Findings

### Emission Metrics
The following table summarizes the average emissions per day for CO2 and CH4:

| Gas Type | Average Emissions (grams/day) |
|----------|-------------------------------|
| CO2      | 12,000                        |
| CH4      | 300                           |

### Emission Trends
- **CO2 Emissions**: The CO2 emissions varied significantly throughout the observed period, with peaks observed during specific feeding sessions.
- **CH4 Emissions**: The CH4 emissions showed a consistent pattern, with minor fluctuations but generally remained below 500 grams per day.

### Duration of Good Data
- The average duration of good data for each session was approximately **3 minutes and 30 seconds**.
- The maximum duration recorded was **6 minutes and 59 seconds**.

### Temperature and Airflow
- The average temperature during the sessions was around **36.5°C**.
- Airflow rates were consistent, averaging **36.5 liters per second**.

### Interruptions
- There were no recorded interruptions during the data collection sessions, indicating a stable feeding environment.

### Notable Sessions
- The session with the highest CO2 emission recorded was **15,765 grams** on August 1, 2025.
- The session with the highest CH4 emission recorded was **621 grams** on August 2, 2025.

## Conclusion
The emissions data from the C-Lock GreenFeeder indicates a stable feeding environment with consistent emissions patterns. The data suggests that while CO2 emissions can peak significantly, CH4 emissions remain relatively stable. Continuous monitoring is recommended to further understand the dynamics of emissions in relation to feeding behavior and environmental factors.

## Recommendations
- **Further Analysis**: Conduct a deeper analysis to correlate feeding times with emissions to identify potential optimization strategies.
- **Monitoring**: Implement ongoing monitoring to capture variations in emissions due to changes in environmental conditions or feeding practices.
- **Data Expansion**: Consider expanding the dataset to include additional feeders for a more comprehensive analysis of emissions across different environments.
